# RAG chunking v1: which chunking retrieves best, whether query type matters, and whether to build a router

Results write-up for the v1 experiment, 2026-10-14. It answers the three questions v1 was built for, from the committed result tables under [`results/`](../results). Terms in bold are defined in [CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md); the design decisions are in [docs/adr](../docs/adr).

## The answers in brief

The experiment asks how to cut documents into pieces (a **chunking**) so that a search system hands a language model the right text. Every chunking gets the same **token budget** (the amount of text the model may read), and the score is **retrieval recall**: the share of the text that answers the question (its **labelled spans**) that ends up inside that budget. A recall of 0.4256 means 42.56% of the answer text was retrieved. The main setting is the **production line** (keyword and vector search fused, then reranked) in the **production view** (at most 8 chunks per question), with search over the whole collection.

1. **Which single chunking retrieves best? It depends on the budget.**
   - With 512 tokens or less, **small-to-big** wins: match small 128-token pieces, then hand over the whole section around a match when it still fits (on Natural Questions alone, section-aware already edges ahead at 512 tokens).
   - At 1024 and 2048 tokens, **section-aware chunking** wins (one chunk per section of the document, split at paragraph boundaries above 512 tokens), on both datasets and with both embedders. At the pre-registered 1024-token budget it retrieves 0.4256 of the answer text pooled over both datasets with bge-small (0.4262 with nomic), and it is the best chunking in 0.9997 of the bootstrap resamples (bge-small); the runner-up, small-to-big, trails it by -0.0174 [-0.0277, -0.0070].
   - At 4096 and 8192 tokens in the production view, the big fixed windows win pooled (fixed-512, then fixed-1024), because the 8-chunk cap stops smaller chunks from using the room. Without the cap (the **fair view**) section-aware stays best at every budget from 1024 up with bge-small; with nomic, fixed-1024 edges ahead at 8192.

2. **Does the best chunk size depend on the kind of question? Not in a way a router could use.**
   - The research pilot found larger chunks losing a little less on context questions (answers spread over several passages) than on precise ones (one passage), but by too little to be sure of. QASPER reproduces exactly that: fixed-512 against fixed-128 at 1024 tokens gives an interaction of +0.0237 [-0.0096, +0.0568] with bge-small, against the pilot's prior of about +0.02 with a CI spanning 0.
   - Natural Questions points the other way: there, bigger windows help precise (short-answer) questions more than context ones, and for fixed-512 at 1024 tokens the CI excludes 0: -0.0647 [-0.1178, -0.0122]. Pooled over both, the interaction disappears: +0.0106 [-0.0190, +0.0410].
   - Most tellingly, section-aware is the best chunking for precise and for context questions alike, so knowing a question's type in hindsight and picking the best chunking for that type gains nothing over section-aware: +0.0000 on every dataset and embedder at 1024 tokens.

3. **Is a query router worth building? Yes, by the rule fixed before any result was seen.**
   - The rule ([ADR 0002](../docs/adr/0002-build-a-query-router-only-if-the-routing-headroom-threshold-is-met.md)): build a router in v2 if, on the production line, in the production view, at 1024 tokens, pooled over QASPER and Natural Questions, either embedder's **routing headroom** (an **oracle router** that picks the best chunking per question in hindsight, minus the **best single chunking**) is at least +0.05 retrieval recall with a 95% confidence interval that excludes 0.
   - **bge-small: +0.1295 [+0.1187, +0.1402]** over section-aware. It clears the gate.
   - **nomic: +0.1313 [+0.1204, +0.1425]** over section-aware. It clears the gate.
   - **Verdict: build the router in v2.** The headroom is the hindsight oracle's, an upper bound no real router reaches, and answer 2 shows it does not come from the precise/context split: it lives in per-question differences that a router has to predict from the question text.

## What v2 should do

The gate cleared for both embedders, so under ADR 0002 v2 builds the query router. It makes a **routing decision** from the query text alone among the seven v1 chunkings, all of them indexed, on the production line, and it is judged where the gate was judged: production view, 1024 tokens, QASPER and Natural Questions pooled, with the same paired, document-clustered bootstrap. Its baseline is the best single chunking, section-aware (0.4256 with bge-small, 0.4262 with nomic), and its ceiling is the oracle router (0.5552 and 0.5575). Because the query-type oracle gains +0.0000 over section-aware, a router that only tells precise from context questions cannot beat the baseline; it has to predict which chunking suits each individual question. v2 should fix its own success bar before it runs, as v1 fixed the gate, and keep section-aware as the single chunking if no router clears that bar. The work [ADR 0004](../docs/adr/0004-run-v1-on-the-settled-question-sets-and-chunking-grid-superseding-the-reports-proposal.md) deferred to v2 (LLM context prefixes, late chunking and answer accuracy) comes on top.

## The question and the design

The goal is a retrieval system that indexes several chunkings and routes each query to the one that suits it. Before building that router, v1 measures three things: the best single chunking, the chunk size × **query type** interaction, and the routing headroom of an oracle router over the best single chunking, with a build-or-not gate fixed in advance. The background and the literature are in the [research report](2026-10-10-chunk-size-vs-query-type.md); the settled design is in four decision records:

- [ADR 0001](../docs/adr/0001-compare-chunkings-at-a-fixed-budget-of-whole-chunks.md): chunkings are compared at a fixed token budget of whole chunks (never cut), scored against labelled spans, in two views: the production view caps the reader at the top 8 chunks (the **top-N cap**), the fair view drops the cap. Decisions are judged on the production view.
- [ADR 0002](../docs/adr/0002-build-a-query-router-only-if-the-routing-headroom-threshold-is-met.md): the pre-registered router gate (+0.05 routing headroom, 95% CI excluding 0, bootstrap resampling documents).
- [ADR 0003](../docs/adr/0003-mirror-the-sister-projects-search-stack.md): the search stack, including its 2026-10-14 amendment (metrics and bootstrap implemented in the project).
- [ADR 0004](../docs/adr/0004-run-v1-on-the-settled-question-sets-and-chunking-grid-superseding-the-reports-proposal.md): the datasets and the chunking grid, superseding the research report's proposal.

**Data.** QASPER (questions about NLP research papers, answered from the paper) and a 1,000-question subset of Natural Questions (questions from search logs, answered from a Wikipedia page, half with a short answer). A question's query type comes from its labels in hindsight and is never available to a router: in Natural Questions a short answer means precise and a long-answer-only question means context; in QASPER precise means every annotator cites one passage, context that every annotator cites several, and mixed that they disagree.

**Chunkings.** Fixed windows of 64, 128, 256, 512 and 1024 tokens (no overlap); section-aware (one chunk per section, split at paragraph boundaries above 512 tokens); small-to-big (128-token children matched, their section-aware parent returned when it fits the room left in the budget). Tokens are counted with one tokenizer, tiktoken `cl100k_base`. Budgets run from 128 to 8192 tokens; a fixed chunk bigger than the budget is not scored at that budget.

**Retrieval.** The production line fuses BM25 keyword search and vector search by reciprocal rank fusion (k = 60), then gte-reranker-modernbert-base reorders the fused top 30; the list continues in fused order after that (the unreranked tail). It runs once per embedder: bge-small-en-v1.5 (reads 512 tokens) and nomic-embed-text-v1.5 (reads 8192). Three **ablations** remove parts of it: hybrid without the reranker, vector search only, and BM25 only. The main results use **collection search** (every document of the dataset at once); search within the question's own document is secondary.

**Statistics.** Routing headroom, the best single chunking and the interaction come with a paired bootstrap that resamples documents (10,000 resamples, seed 0), because questions about the same document are not independent. The best single chunking is re-selected inside every resample. CIs are 95% at the pre-registered 1024-token budget and Holm-adjusted across the other budgets (the CI level column gives the level used). "Pooled" in R7-R9 means the per-question results of the QASPER and Natural Questions runs taken together, each dataset's documents resampled as its own clusters.

Reading the tables:

- **Small budgets favour small-to-big; 1024-2048 tokens favour section-aware.** Section-aware chunks are up to 512 tokens long, so at 128 and 256 tokens few of them fit and section-aware is among the worst; small-to-big has the same parents but falls back to its 128-token child when the parent does not fit, which makes it the best choice while the budget is tight.
- **The production view's cap decides the large budgets.** From 2048 tokens the 8-chunk cap starts to bind for small and section-sized chunks (the † cells in R2), so in the production view the big fixed windows catch up and, from 4096 tokens, lead; the fair view shows section-aware still ahead when the cap is lifted.
- **The two embedders agree.** The best chunking is the same for bge-small and nomic in every cell but three (NQ at 4096 tokens in the production view; QASPER and pooled at 8192 tokens in the fair view), and the best recalls stay within about 0.01 of each other.
- **QASPER is hard because of collection search, not chunking.** Finding the right paper among hundreds of papers on similar topics is the main loss: within the question's own paper, the same pipeline retrieves about three times as much of the answer text.

## 2. Chunk size × query type, against the research pilot's prior

The question: do bigger chunks suit context questions and smaller chunks precise ones? The interaction measures it. For a chunking against fixed-128, take its gain on context questions minus its gain on precise questions; a positive interaction means the chunking does relatively better on context questions.

**What the pilot predicted.** The research report's pilot (BM25 within each paper, word windows) found the effect pointing the expected way but too small to be sure of, and its prior for v1 was an interaction of about +0.02 with a CI spanning 0 for fixed-512 against fixed-128 at 1024 tokens. The report proposed calling the hypothesis supported only if the CI excluded 0 on at least two datasets and two embedders, and rejected in practical terms if the CI lay entirely below +0.03.

## 5. Limitations

- **Truncation under bge-small.** bge-small reads at most 512 tokens, so a longer chunk is embedded from its beginning only. Most fixed-512 and fixed-1024 chunks on QASPER are truncated (and most fixed-1024 chunks on Natural Questions); few section-aware chunks are. nomic reads 8192 tokens and truncates nothing, yet the production line's recall with it is within a few thousandths of bge-small's for every truncated chunking, so truncation does not move any conclusion here.

- **Cells that used the unreranked tail.** The reranker orders only the fused top 30. Packing skips a chunk that does not fit the room left in the budget and moves on, so with section-sized chunks (up to 512 tokens) it often reaches past rank 30, where chunks stay in fused order; small-to-big also skips children whose parent is already packed. The two winning chunkings at 1024 tokens rely on the tail most: section-aware used it for 0.5321 of QASPER and 0.8010 of Natural Questions questions (bge-small). The tables mark every such cell with §. A deeper rerank could change their margin over the fixed windows in either direction.

- **Annotator disagreement and the mixed query type.** QASPER annotators often disagree on whether a question needs one passage or several. The design added a third query type, mixed, for those questions (476 of 2197): they count in every mean and in the gate, but are left out of the interaction. Recall keeps the annotator whose spans were retrieved best, a generous reading when annotators disagree. Natural Questions' subset keeps questions where at least 2 of 5 annotators gave a paragraph long answer, and its precise questions are scored against that paragraph like its context ones (R6 scores the short answers).
- **Short papers.** QASPER papers are short, so within a paper the large budgets hold nearly everything: at 8192 tokens a within-document search retrieves 0.9980 to 0.9999 of the answer text with every chunking (fair view; Natural Questions 0.9633 to 0.9960), and those budgets cannot separate chunkings there. The collection-search results above do not have this ceiling.
- **The combined-collection tables are BM25-only.** The production line was not run on one index holding both datasets, so [`results/pooled/`](../results/pooled) has BM25 tables only. This does not touch the gate or anything called "pooled" in R7-R9, which combine the per-question results of the two per-dataset production runs.
- **Deterministic tie handling was added after the first runs.** Searches now fetch past each cutoff so tied scores cannot change which chunks are kept ([pull request 14](https://github.com/lbildzinkas/rag-findings/pull/14)). Every dataset was re-run; a handful of fixed-64 cells under bge-small moved in the fourth decimal (listed in that pull request), and every table here comes from the re-runs.
- **Metrics and the bootstrap are implemented in the project, not with ranx.** ADR 0003 named ranx, but ranx scores document-level relevance, not token overlap with labelled spans at a fixed budget; the amendment to ADR 0003 records the change.
- **The oracle is an upper bound.** It takes the best of seven chunkings for every question in hindsight, so part of any headroom is noise; ties go to the smaller chunk. One run per pipeline, one reranker depth (30), one fusion constant (k = 60), retrieval only (no answer accuracy), and English text from two sources: NLP papers and Wikipedia.
