# Pre-register v2's success bar and the tie-break between contenders

v1 fixed its gate before seeing any result (ADR 0002) and v2 keeps that discipline, with one addition: several contenders now compete for one verdict, so both the bar and the choice between contenders that clear it are fixed before any v2 run, or the winner would be picked by noise.

A contender passes if its retrieval recall beats section-aware by at least +0.02 in the budget view at 1024 tokens, pooled over QASPER and Natural Questions, with a 95% confidence interval from the paired, document-clustered bootstrap excluding 0; if it is no worse than section-aware by more than 0.01 on either dataset alone; and if its answer score is no worse than section-aware's by more than 0.01, that bound checked the same way. If several contenders pass, the one with the highest retrieval recall wins, unless a simpler one is within 0.01 of it - simplest first: late chunking, confidence routing, search-all routing, contextual chunks. If none passes, section-aware stays. The alternatives were judging in v1's production view, whose cap of 8 handicaps small chunks (ADR 0006); letting the answer score decide alone, when answers are the noisier measure and retrieval is the quantity the contenders differ on; and picking the winner by the highest pooled recall with no tie-break, which would hand a within-noise gain to the most complex setup.

## Amendment (2026-10-17): the answer guard no longer applies

No contender reached the retrieval part of the bar: every one scored below section-aware in pooled retrieval recall at 1,024 tokens in the budget view, the closest being contextual chunks at 0.0179 below. The answer-score guard (no worse than section-aware by more than 0.01) only ever decided between contenders that pass on retrieval, so it is dropped, and the reader stage reads section-aware, the oracle router and no retrieval only (see the amendment to ADR 0009). The bar and the tie-break above are unchanged, and the formal verdict against them, with its confidence intervals, is still to be judged.
